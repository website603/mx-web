The Problem

You Know What Responsible Leadership Looks Like.
Getting There Is Another Conversation.

You have spent years building expertise, earning authority, and developing a clear sense of what good leadership actually looks like. You care deeply about the people affected by what you build. You think about consequences that stretch well beyond the quarterly cycle.

And most days, the speed of the work makes it genuinely hard to act on any of that.

Our research with 25 global leaders across AI, consulting, academia, and social impact found that the gap almost everyone feels is surprisingly consistent. Leaders across every sector hold strong ethical values. What they lack are the structures, the practices, and the peer community that would allow them to act on those values with the same consistency they bring to everything else.

The Responsible Leadership Retreat exists to help close that gap.

The Experience

Five Days of Work That
Actually Changes Something

The Responsible Leadership Retreat brings together 20 senior leaders for five days of deep, structured work in Bali. It is built around three pillars that mirror the real shape of responsible leadership in the AI era — the inner work, the design work, and the long-view work.

Each pillar draws on a distinct set of methodologies, brought by facilitators who have spent years developing and applying them. You will leave with concrete tools, individual maps built from your own professional context, a personal AI readiness assessment, and a cohort of peers who have done the same work alongside you.

Methodologies — InnerGuidance Method · Design and Systems Thinking · AI Governance Frameworks · Futures Scoping

Responsible Mindset

Responsible leadership starts from the inside. How clearly you see yourself. How steadily you can hold your values under pressure. How much access you have to your own judgment when everything around you is moving fast.

This pillar works with the InnerGuidance Method — drawing from modern communication science, somatic awareness, and contemplative wisdom traditions — to help you develop relational intelligence, embodied clarity, and the courage to lead from your actual values rather than the version of yourself your institution expects.

The Bali environment is an intentional part of this work. Guided reflection in nature, morning practices on the land, and evening conversations that the setting makes possible.

You will leave with
  • The capacity to pause, read a situation clearly, and choose actions that honour human dignity
  • The courage to act on those choices in rooms where it takes real strength
  • Your individual Somatic and Relational Intelligence Profile

Responsible Tech

The most consequential decisions in an AI organisation are made upstream — in product scope, in dataset selection, in the metrics a model is trained to optimise. By the time those decisions reach a governance review, the architecture is already set.

This pillar uses Design Thinking and Systems Thinking frameworks alongside established AI Governance frameworks to help you get present in the decisions that actually shape outcomes. You will work with real use cases from your own organisation, in small groups with practitioners who have done this work at scale.

You will leave with
  • A Responsible AI System Map from one real use case in your work
  • Design principles you can defend technically and ethically
  • A shared language for naming and challenging irresponsible decisions in the rooms where they are made
  • Practical tools to operationalise responsible AI without slowing the pace of your work

Responsible Futures

Every organisation is building a future — in its data practices, its product decisions, its incentive structures, and the assumptions embedded in how it measures success. This pillar uses Futures Scoping methodology to help you see that future clearly, map its consequences, and develop the strategic language and personal authority to shape it with intention.

The work is applied directly to your organisational context and built collaboratively with your cohort, so the scenarios you leave with are grounded in real decisions you will face.

You will leave with
  • A Responsible Futures Map for your organisation or domain
  • Shared scenarios built directly from your strategic context
  • The language to argue for long-term responsibility in rooms driven by short-term pressure
  • A clearer sense of where your professional authority and personal purpose meet

Your Individual Output

Leave with a Clear Picture
of Where You Stand

Before the retreat, every participant completes a structured pre-work assessment. Over the five days, your facilitators build on this alongside what emerges in the room.

At the close of the retreat, each participant receives a personal AI Readiness Report — a structured, individual assessment of how your purpose, your people leadership, and the products you shape are currently positioned on the journey toward responsible AI.

This is not a score or a ranking. It is a clear-eyed picture of where genuine readiness exists, where the gaps are, and where the highest-leverage opportunities for change sit in your specific context.

01

Purpose Readiness

How well your stated mission and personal leadership values are reflected in the AI decisions being made in your organisation. Where clarity exists, and where the gap between aspiration and practice is widest.

02

People Readiness

How prepared your teams are to build and govern AI responsibly — their awareness, their capacity for ethical judgment, and the relational infrastructure that makes speaking up possible.

03

Product Readiness

How the systems, products, and processes you oversee reflect the principles of responsible AI — in design, in governance, and in the accountability structures around them.

"The report is prepared by your facilitation team and shared privately. It is yours to use as a working document, not a public assessment."

The Programme

A Day in the Retreat

Each day in Bali follows a deliberate rhythm. Mornings carry the stillness that deep work needs. Afternoons move into collaborative and applied sessions. Evenings are held lightly — for reflection, for conversation, and for the kind of connection that only happens when people are genuinely present together in a beautiful place.

01 Day One

Arrival and Orientation

You arrive in Bali and the work begins gently. Opening ceremony, cohort introductions, and a shared first conversation about what each person is carrying into the week. A dinner in the rice terraces as the sun goes down — the first evening of what most participants describe as a week that felt different from the first night.

02 Day Two

Responsible Mindset

Morning somatic and reflective practice in nature, drawing on the InnerGuidance Method. Facilitated sessions on leadership identity, inner clarity, and discernment. Afternoon work in small groups on the ethical judgment scenarios most present for each participant. Guided practice at dusk.

03 Day Three

Responsible Tech

Design and Systems Thinking workshop applied to AI governance. Each participant maps a real use case from their own work through an ethical design lens, using AI Governance frameworks. Peer challenge sessions in the afternoon. The evening is open — Bali at night does the rest.

04 Day Four

Responsible Futures

Futures Scoping methodology, applied to each participant's organisational context. Scenario building in small groups. Stress-testing strategic decisions against the futures you are collectively building. Community dinner and a conversation that tends to go late.

05 Day Five

Integration and What Comes Next

The morning is for integration — drawing threads together, building personal leadership commitments, and pairing with a cohort peer for ongoing accountability. Each participant receives their individual AI Readiness Report. Closing ceremony. Departure breakfast. The conversation that continues after you leave.

Is This For You?

Who This Retreat Is For

This is for you if

  • You are at Director level or above, with genuine influence over AI strategy, product decisions, or organisational direction — the kind of influence where your choices compound.
  • You feel the weight of what you are building. Not as anxiety to manage, but as a real sense of responsibility for the people and systems your decisions touch.
  • You hold strong values around responsible AI and you are looking for the practical conditions, the methodology, and the peer community to act on them more consistently.
  • You want to develop somatic and relational intelligence alongside strategic frameworks — the human capacities that make responsible leadership possible in practice, not just in principle.
  • You want to be the person in the room who can name what is going wrong, hold the tension, and have the standing and the skill to move things differently.
  • You are ready to give five uninterrupted days to this work. Fully present — phone down, calendar cleared, genuinely here.
  • You know that responsible AI matters, and you are honest with yourself that you do not yet have a coherent way to embed it in your daily work. That is exactly what this is for.

This is a strong fit when

  • You have eight or more years of leadership experience and you are operating at a level where your decisions shape not just your team but the broader direction of what your organisation builds.
  • You are genuinely curious about the people and communities affected by what you build, alongside the systems and technologies themselves.
  • You can commit to five full days in Bali in August 2026 — including the pre-retreat preparation work and the post-retreat integration calls.
  • You are open to working with your body and your inner life alongside your intellect. The methodologies we use are whole-person.

Our Research

The Responsible Leadership
Gap Report

Most leaders in our study are clear about what responsible leadership looks like. They believe in it. They would choose people over efficiency, ethics over expedience, long-term thinking over short-term gain — given the chance.

The gap is structural. It lives between what leaders believe in private and what their organisations make possible in practice.

Our pilot study with 25 global leaders across AI, consulting, academia, and social impact mapped that gap in detail. The findings are specific, grounded in real voices, and directly relevant to anyone leading in the AI era.

What is inside

  • The Three-Layer Gap Model — how external pressure, internal erosion, and structural absence work together to produce the coherence crisis leaders feel
  • Five named gaps, with data and direct leader voices from across the global study
  • Three additions written specifically for AI leaders: a decision-speed taxonomy, the responsible-by-design framework, and an honest account of what the gap is actually costing organisations
  • Practitioner implications grounded directly in what the data shows — things you can act on this week
